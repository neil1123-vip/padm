A little space

Making room for questions

3 October 2026 · 3 minute read

A notebook can become crowded with answers before it has made enough room for questions. The result looks productive, but it leaves little space for the uncertainty that often starts useful work.

Reserve a few pages for questions that do not belong to an article yet. They can be fragments, reminders or things that sounded strange for a moment and then disappeared.

Collect without solving

Write the question in the smallest form that still makes sense. “Why did the room feel larger after the chairs moved?” is easier to revisit than a paragraph that tries to explain it immediately.

  • Keep the date and the place where the question appeared.
  • Add one concrete detail that made you ask it.
  • Do not assign a subject until a pattern begins to form.

Several questions may eventually point to the same note. Until then, their shared page is enough.

Return by association

Read the question list after writing something unrelated. A phrase in a new note may give an older question a useful neighbour. Add a link or a page number rather than moving the original question.

This creates a record of how ideas meet. The first question stays tied to its setting, while the later connection remains visible as a separate event.

Questions do not need to be urgent to deserve a place.

Close a question carefully

When an answer arrives, write it below the question and keep the original wording. Sometimes the answer changes the question rather than ending it. Mark that change with a date and continue on a new page.

A small reserve of open space keeps a notebook responsive. It lets a passing observation wait long enough to show whether it belongs to a larger pattern.
