High ground

Reading a ridgeline

22 July 2025

A mountain is a mass of small planes. Snow and shadow let the eye follow their meeting points.

Start at the skyline

The line where rock meets sky is one of the clearest boundaries in the photograph. It makes the distant mountains easy to read at a glance, even at a small size. From there, the eye follows the lighter channels down into the valley.

The snow is useful because it describes the form rather than covering it. Narrow strips sit in the folds. They suggest the direction of the slopes and show where one surface turns away from another.

Leave the subject some air

A tight crop can make a mountain feel large, but it can also make it difficult to understand. Some sky around the summits gives the outlines room. At the bottom, enough forested foreground remains to show that the ridges belong to a larger landscape.

The clouds have softer edges than the mountains. The foreground trees have more texture but occupy a different plane. These differences let the eye move across the scene without losing the line of the distant ridge.

Scale without a person

People are often used to establish scale in landscape photographs. Here, there is no person to measure against. Instead, scale comes from layers: the small ridges within the main slope, the changes in texture and the distance implied by the overlapping forms.

Spend a moment looking at the smaller triangles within the slopes. They repeat the shape of the larger ridges without being identical. The valley draws those layers together and gives the eye a route into the distance.

Look at a smaller version

At a reduced viewing size, the fine texture becomes less important and the larger bands of forest, rock and sky remain. This makes a useful second reading: does the valley still give the eye a way through the photograph when the smallest details are difficult to see?

Return to the full image afterwards. The purpose is to understand which shapes carry the composition, not to judge every photograph by how quickly it works as a thumbnail.
